# V4.1 campaign review

Campaign `v41-463e8d1eca6867a9` is the current paper-aligned research output under `campaign_v41_verified/`. The publication gate passed and independently replayed all selected exact genotypes against the current code and site state. The manifest hashes 159 generated datasets and figures. The formal Figure 1/Figure 4 parity report records 21 running stages with explicit inputs, outputs and provenance.

The 29-run campaign archived 4,736 evaluated births; 3,571 satisfy the model's hard constraints. Feasible phenotypes span 8–32 residents. None has a positive-area same-floor overlap or an unpermitted shared-edge contact. Among feasible archive records, 2,332 have at least one intentional shared-edge assembly and 702 contain a separately checked stacked footprint. Detached, linear, courtyard, wing and mixed patterns all appear. The retained variational-module fraction spans 0–0.625; balanced, dominated and total zoning states all appear. These are archive counts, not counts of independent built designs.

The three base runs each end with 32 feasible residential candidates. They record 431, 373 and 409 distinct architectural signatures, respectively, so none triggered the defined search-space-collapse flag. The five selection runs each produce 11 mode labels, resolving to 9–10 distinct phenotype sheets per run. Pareto representatives, per-objective fittest, relative-difference, average-fitness-rank and architectural balanced modes therefore show different valid trade-offs where the search space supports them.

All 24 capacity strata (8–48 concurrent day users crossed with 8, 16, 24 and 32 residents) found a model-feasible candidate under their recorded search budgets. This is a computational reachability observation, not evidence of regulatory or operational feasibility. Nominal room capacity remains a separate figure from the one allocated concurrent day-user population.

For equal random seeds, every genome birth in the 32-user and 48-user base runs is identical (544/544 for seed 42 and 544/544 for seed 7). Their occupancy allocations and phenotype IDs differ by scenario, but all tested room schedules had sufficient nominal capacity, so the higher day-user demand did not change ranking or geometric evolution. The 48-user runs therefore test allocation and model feasibility; they are not separately optimized 48-user plans. A later architectural study should add evidence-based peak-use, congestion and room-sharing criteria before claiming demand-responsive geometry.

The figures are schematic spatial-programming studies. Exact gate and service access, environmental nuisance, flood/drainage, vegetation, growth direction, route widths, structural stacking and care operation remain to be verified. The algorithm does not author the PA5 scheme; the archive supplies traceable alternatives for architectural plan and section development.
